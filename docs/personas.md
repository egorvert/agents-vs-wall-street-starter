## Core foundational research lenses

| Lens | Exclusive question | Expected extraction |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Metric and historical custodian | What exactly are we forecasting, and how has it behaved historically? | Metric definition, GAAP/adjusted treatment, fiscal calendar, 8–12 periods of history, seasonality, prior surprises and restatements |
| 2. Management guidance analyst | What has management explicitly said about the target period? | Formal guidance ranges, revisions, assumptions, superseded guidance, management KPIs and cited risks |
| 3. Operating-model analyst | What operational variables mathematically produce the target? | Volume, price, mix, headcount, utilization, segment revenue, margins, tax and shares—depending on the company |
| 4. Demand and customer analyst | What is happening to underlying end demand? | Customer activity, category growth, orders, bookings, traffic, hiring demand or farm purchasing power |
| 5. Supply, cost and inventory analyst | What constrains delivery and profitability? | Capacity, backlog, lead times, channel inventory, dealer inventory, input costs, procurement, labor and logistics |
| 6. Peer and ecosystem analyst | What can customers, suppliers, distributors and competitors tell us? | Direct read-across, comparable KPIs, market-share changes and contradictory peer evidence |
| 7. Macro, policy and external-forces analyst | Which external variables materially affect this specific quarter? | Rates, FX, employment, housing, commodities, tariffs, subsidies, regulation and regional economic data |
| 8. Current-quarter nowcast analyst | What changed after the last company report or consensus update? | Recent monthly data, intra-quarter indicators, corporate events, weather/calendar effects and post-consensus information |
| 9. Street-expectations analyst | What exactly is priced into analyst expectations? | Consensus, high/low range, dispersion, analyst count, revision direction, snapshot date and metric-definition differences |
| 10. Accounting and capital-structure analyst | What could cause the reported number to differ from the operating story? | Adjustments, restructuring, acquisition accounting, FX translation, tax, interest, diluted shares, buybacks and one-offs |

This gives you distinct source territories while covering the complete path from historical baseline to reported financial result.

## Conditional specialists

Spawn these only when a routing check determines they are material:

- Product and technology-cycle specialist: product launches, semiconductor cycles, obsolescence, design wins.
- M&A and integration specialist: acquired revenue, purchase accounting, synergies and divestitures.
- Regulatory and litigation specialist: settlements, compliance costs, legal restrictions and right-to-repair issues.
- Geopolitical and trade specialist: tariffs, export controls, sanctions and cross-border sourcing.
- Weather and calendar specialist: storms, extreme temperatures, working days, holiday timing and 53-week effects.
- Market-implied expectations specialist: options-implied volatility and price reactions. Useful for uncertainty, but generally weak evidence for the exact earnings number.

## Reframe some of your proposed personas

Several proposed personas are better used as reasoning passes rather than foundational researchers:

- Hedge fund and buy-side analyst → combine into a **variant-perception analyst** after evidence collection. Its question is: “Where and why should we disagree with consensus?”
- Bull and bear agents → use as a counterargument pass over the same accepted evidence, not as separate fact collectors.
- Short-term spot trader → redefine as the current-quarter nowcast analyst.
- Sell-side analyst → narrowly own consensus, estimate revisions and metric conventions.
- Fundamental analyst → split across the historical, guidance and operating-model lenses.

This prevents multiple agents from independently producing slightly different versions of the same fundamental report.

## Required output from every research lens

Every agent should return the same compact packet:

- Three to five atomic claims
- Exact affected metric or metrics
- Fact versus inference classification
- Direction and causal driver
- Publication date and information cutoff
- Source and supporting excerpt
- Any numerical fact with unit and period
- Strongest counterevidence
- What changed relative to the previous quarter or consensus snapshot
- What would falsify the conclusion
- No final forecast value

That last constraint is important: foundational agents should provide evidence and causal direction, not twelve competing final forecasts.

## Completeness gate

Instead of asking whether you ran enough personas, check whether every metric has coverage across five dimensions:

1. Historical baseline and definition  
2. Management guidance  
3. Operating driver bridge  
4. Independent external corroboration  
5. Street expectation and counterevidence  

If one dimension is empty, the research orchestrator should either commission a targeted follow-up or explicitly mark it unavailable. That is a much stronger definition of “comprehensive” than simply increasing the number of personas.
