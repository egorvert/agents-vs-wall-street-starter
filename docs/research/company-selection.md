### 1.3 Data-quality warning — read before trusting any consensus number above

The free aggregators mix **GAAP and non-GAAP/adjusted** figures *inside the same column*. Two proven examples
found tonight:

- **Oracle**: Sharemaestro lists a Q1 FY27 consensus of **$1.39**. Oracle's own non-GAAP guidance is
  **$1.72–$1.76** and LSEG consensus was **$1.68**. The $1.39 is a GAAP-basis number. Using it would produce a
  catastrophically wrong "surprise".
- **Accenture**: MarketBeat lists Q4 FY25 actual EPS as $3.03 (adjusted) while its FY-derived GAAP number is
  $2.26 — a $0.78 business-optimisation charge sitting between them.

**Rule for tomorrow:** the reported/consensus basis must be sourced from *the same* provider for both actual and
estimate, and must match the basis the company headlines. Anchor on the company's own guidance language
("core diluted EPS", "non-GAAP EPS", "adjusted diluted EPS"), then find the consensus quoted on that basis
(Reuters/LSEG numbers in CNBC or Reuters earnings stories are the cheapest reliable cross-check).

## 2. Predictability scoring framework

### 2.1 The six criteria, weighted

| # | Criterion | Why it matters | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | **Explicit quarterly guidance** | Single strongest predictor. A company that guides revenue and EPS for the *exact* quarter has told you the answer to ±2–3%. | **35%** |
| b | **Surprise stability** (stdev of EPS surprise %, 8 qtrs) | Low stdev ⇒ your residual after conditioning on guidance is small. | 20% |
| c | **Business-model stability** (recurring/subscription vs cyclical/commodity) | Deferred-revenue and contracted backlog businesses are near-deterministic; commodity/cyclical ones are not. | 15% |
| d | **Intra-quarter observable data** | Monthly sales, unit deliveries, backlog, RPO — actual realised data covering part of the quarter you're forecasting. | 15% |
| e | **Segment-disclosure richness** | Lets you build bottom-up rather than guess a single number. | 8% |
| f | **Coverage depth / dispersion** | Deep coverage ⇒ tight consensus ⇒ hard to beat but easy to match. Thin coverage ⇒ the opposite. | 7% |

A **disqualifier** overrides all of the above: recent M&A close, spin-off, segment recut, fiscal-year change,
53rd week, or accounting-basis change in the forecast quarter.

## 3. Where consensus is most beatable — and the strategy tension

### 3.1 What the surprise literature actually says

Four robust, well-replicated regularities, and what each implies here:

1. **The optimism walk-down.** Analysts start a quarter optimistic and are guided down into the print, so
   ~70–75% of S&P 500 companies beat EPS consensus in a typical quarter. **Implication:** "consensus + a small
   positive nudge" is a ~70% base-rate winner on *direction*, before any modelling. This is your floor, not
   your strategy.
2. **Guidance dominates.** Where management issues point/range guidance for the exact quarter, actuals cluster
   at or just above the range midpoint, and analysts anchor there too. **Implication:** for guiders, both you
   and consensus will be close; your edge comes from modelling *where inside the range*, not from a different
   view.
3. **Consensus error rises with**: low analyst coverage, high forecast dispersion, high earnings volatility,
   loss-making or near-zero-EPS firms, recent restructuring/M&A/segment changes, and long horizons since the
   last guidance update. **Implication:** these are where consensus is beatable.
4. **Low coverage cuts both ways.** With 3 analysts, the "consensus" is a noisy 3-point mean; beating it is
   partly luck, and the measured actual-vs-consensus gap is itself noisy. **Implication:** thin-coverage
   names raise *your* variance as much as consensus's.

### 3.2 The tension, stated precisely

The contest does not score |your error|. It scores |your error| **relative to** |consensus error|. Define:

```
Edge = |consensus_error| - |your_error|
```

Two failure modes:

- **The "safe" trap (Darden, Costco, FactSet).** Consensus error is tiny (Darden's mean EPS surprise is
  **−0.75%** with σ 1.6%; Costco's revenue σ is **0.45%**). You will also be accurate — but so is consensus,
  so Edge ≈ 0 and the outcome is a coin flip on rounding. Great if scoring is absolute error. Worthless if
  scoring is "did you beat the Street".
- **The "lottery" trap (CarMax, Constellation, KB Home).** Consensus error is large (KMX EPS surprise σ = 32%),
  but so is yours, because the thing that makes consensus wrong (credit-loss provisions, used-car pricing) is
  exactly what you can't model in a day. Edge has huge variance and ~zero mean.

**The sweet spot is neither.** It is: **large, persistent, directional consensus bias + small residual
conditional on public guidance.** That is, companies where the Street is *predictably* wrong in a *known
direction* and the company has *told you* the answer.

**Answer to "what maximises P(beat consensus)":** not maximum uncertainty, and not maximum predictability.
It is **maximum (consensus bias ÷ your residual σ)**. On the evidence gathered, that ranking is
**JBL > MU > ADBE ≈ ORCL > AZO > COST > ACN > CTAS > LEN > DRI**.

### 3.4 The EPS-denominator trap (important, easy to get wrong)

If the metric is **percentage** error on EPS, companies with small absolute EPS are landmines. Nike's mean EPS
surprise is **+50%** — not because Nike is unpredictable, but because its quarterly EPS is $0.20–0.50, so a
$0.09 beat is +82%. Carnival's is worse (+550% in one quarter). Conversely AutoZone's EPS is ~$38, so a $1.85
beat is only +5%.

- If scored on **% error**: avoid NKE, CCL, KBH, LEVI, VRA, and anything with EPS < ~$1.00. Favour high-EPS,
  low-volatility names (AZO, MU, FDS, ACN, ADBE, COST).
- If scored on **absolute $ error**: the reverse — small-EPS names are safe and $38-EPS AutoZone is dangerous.
- If scored on **error vs consensus error** (most likely): denominator matters less, but still confirm.

**Ask the organisers which metric before locking the shortlist.** This single question changes the answer more
than any modelling work you'll do tomorrow.

## 5. Multi-company portfolio logic

### 5.1 How many to submit

**Submit 4–5 if allowed. Not 1, not 10.**

**If scoring is "fraction of names where you beat consensus":** treat each name as an independent Bernoulli
trial. Using a realistic per-name win probability of p ≈ 0.7 (achievable with the guidance-anchored approach):

| N names | P(win majority) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0.700 |
| 3 | 0.784 |
| 5 | **0.837** |
| 7 | 0.874 |

Diminishing returns set in fast after 5, and each additional name costs build + verification time that would
otherwise go into making each forecast better (raising p, which helps more than raising N).

**If scoring is "mean absolute error across names":** the standard error of the mean scales as σ/√N. Going from
1 → 4 names **halves** the dispersion of your score. Same conclusion.

**If scoring is "best single forecast wins" (winner-take-all on one number):** the logic inverts entirely — you
want *maximum upside variance*, so you'd pick one high-dispersion name and swing (Micron). **Establish which
regime you're in before committing.** This is the second question to ask the organisers.

### 5.2 Correlation is the thing that kills portfolios

Naive picks cluster: Micron + Jabil + Broadcom + Oracle are all levered to the same AI-capex cycle. One demand
datapoint moves all four the same way, and your "diversified" portfolio is a single bet with extra steps.

Enforce **one name per macro driver**:

| Driver | Representative | Alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| AI / memory capex | MU | AVGO |
| Enterprise software renewals | ADBE | ORCL, CRM |
| US consumer staples traffic | COST | KR, GIS |
| US housing / rates | LEN | KBH |
| Corporate services / employment | ACN | CTAS, PAYX, FDS |
| Contract manufacturing | JBL | SNX |

### 5.3 Also diversify *failure mode*, not just sector

- **Guidance-anchored** (ADBE, ORCL, MU, JBL) — fails if the company misses its own guide.
- **Observed-data-anchored** (COST via monthly sales, LEN via backlog) — fails only on margin/mix, not volume.
- **Bridge-anchored** (ACN, CTAS, DRI via FY-guide arithmetic) — fails if the seasonal split shifts.

A portfolio of 5 spanning all three anchoring methods is genuinely robust; five guidance-anchored names are not.

### 5.4 Practical constraints

- Every additional name is another data-verification burden, and the **GAAP/non-GAAP basis error** (§1.3) is a
  far bigger threat to your score than model quality. Budget ~20 minutes per name purely on basis verification.
- **Forecast both revenue and EPS** on every name. Revenue is systematically easier (revenue surprise σ is
  0.4–1.2% for the good names vs 1.6–9% on EPS) — if the scoring averages the two, revenue is where you bank
  points cheaply.

### The selection algorithm to run tomorrow

Run this in order. It should take under 20 minutes.

```
STEP 0 — ASK THE ORGANISERS (blocking; do this at 09:05)
  Q1: Exact scoring date/deadline? → sets the date filter
  Q2: Metric = absolute error, % error, or error-vs-consensus? → sets §3.4 branch
  Q3: How many companies may we submit? Scored as mean, or best-of?
  Q4: Which consensus source is authoritative, and on what basis (GAAP vs adjusted)?
  Q5: Are non-US reporters allowed?
  → If Q2 = "beat consensus": optimise for bias (JBL, MU, ORCL first).
  → If Q2 = "absolute accuracy": optimise for σ (ADBE, COST, DRI, FDS first).
  → If Q3 = "best single forecast": drop the portfolio, submit MU alone.

STEP 1 — DATE FILTER (hard)
  Keep only: report date ≤ (scoring date − 2 days).
  Verify each surviving date on the company's OWN IR page. Aggregators were
  wrong on ORCL, contradictory on ACN and KMX. Do not skip this.

STEP 2 — DISQUALIFIER FILTER (hard)
  Drop any name with, in or adjacent to the forecast quarter:
    fiscal-year change · spin-off / discontinued ops · segment recut
    · M&A closing · 53rd week · accounting-basis change
  (This is what removed FDX; it is what flags CTAS/UniFirst and ADBE/Semrush.)

STEP 3 — GUIDANCE GATE (hard, unless overridden by Step 4)
  Keep only names where EITHER:
    (a) the company guided revenue AND EPS for this exact quarter, OR
    (b) intra-quarter actuals cover ≥50% of the quarter (COST monthly sales,
        LEN backlog), OR
    (c) an FY guide + a stable seasonal split gives a bridge that TWO independent
        derivations agree on (ACN, DRI, CTAS).

STEP 4 — SCORE AND RANK
  score = 0.35·guidance_precision
        + 0.20·(1 / surprise_stdev)
        + 0.15·model_stability
        + 0.15·intra_quarter_data
        + 0.08·segment_disclosure
        + 0.07·coverage_quality
  Then re-rank by regime:
    if metric == "beat consensus": rank by (|mean surprise bias| / surprise_stdev)
    if metric == "absolute error": rank by (1 / surprise_stdev)
    if metric uses % error:        drop everything with quarterly EPS < $1.00

STEP 5 — PORTFOLIO CONSTRUCTION
  Take the top N (N = 4 or 5) subject to:
    - max 1 name per macro driver (§5.2)
    - at least 2 distinct anchoring methods (guidance / observed-data / bridge)
    - at least 1 non-tech name
    - at least 1 "high-edge / high-variance" name (MU) IF the metric rewards
      beating consensus; drop it if the metric is pure absolute accuracy

STEP 6 — BASIS VERIFICATION (do not skip — highest-ROI 20 min of the day)
  For each selected name, confirm from ONE source that actual and consensus are
  quoted on the SAME basis, matching the company's own headline language
  ("non-GAAP EPS", "core diluted EPS", "adjusted diluted EPS").
  Cross-check against an LSEG/Reuters number quoted in a CNBC or Reuters story.
  A basis error is worth more score than any modelling improvement you can make.

STEP 7 — FORECAST RULE (the baseline the agent must beat)
  Baseline: forecast = guidance_midpoint × (1 + historical_mean_beat_vs_guidance)
  Ship this first as a floor, then let the agent try to improve on it.
  Any agent output that lands OUTSIDE the company's own guidance range must be
  flagged for human review before submission.
```
